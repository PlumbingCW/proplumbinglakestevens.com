200,000 BTU Tankless Water Heater: What It Can Run in Lake Stevens

A 200,000 BTU tankless water heater can deliver roughly 8 gallons per minute at a modest temperature rise and noticeably less when incoming water is cold. The math is simple, and it decides whether one unit covers your home.

The formula behind tankless sizing

Tankless sizing comes down to flow and temperature rise. Temperature rise is the difference between the incoming water temperature and the temperature you want. Flow in gallons per minute is roughly the unit's output BTU divided by 500 times the temperature rise. The 500 comes from the fact that a gallon of water weighs about 8.34 pounds and one BTU heats a pound by one degree, with 60 minutes in an hour.

No unit delivers all its input as heat. A 200,000 BTU input unit with 0.9 efficiency has about 180,000 BTU of output. Use output, not input, in the formula, and check the manufacturer's flow chart for the exact figures on your model.

What a 200,000 BTU unit needs to work

  • Gas line size: a large enough gas line and meter for the full input.
  • Venting: the exact vent material and length the manufacturer allows.
  • Water line size: adequate pipe to carry the flow.
  • Electrical: an outlet for the controls and fan.
  • Condensate drain for condensing models.

How many fixtures can it run at once?

Typical flow rates are about 1.5 to 2.5 gpm for a shower, around 1 to 1.5 gpm for a kitchen sink and a dishwasher or washer using about 1 to 2 gpm. A family running two showers and a dishwasher might need around 6 to 8 gpm. A 200,000 BTU unit covers that in mild incoming water temperatures but may fall short in a cold snap.

If the numbers are tight, a plumber can recommend two smaller units, a recirculation loop for faster hot water or simply limiting simultaneous use during the coldest months. Aerators and low-flow showerheads stretch capacity.

Local note

Sizing for a Lake Stevens winter

Incoming water temperature changes with the season. Winter inlet temperature in western Washington can be notably colder than summer, which shrinks flow at the same BTU. Size for winter, not summer, and ask your plumber to measure the actual incoming temperature at your tap if the numbers are tight.

Snohomish County PUD lists total hardness for the Lake Stevens system between 9 and 89 ppm, averaging 73. That range is modest, but tankless heat exchangers can scale over time, so plan a yearly flush. Natural gas service must be confirmed with your gas utility for the load.

Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments, which affects gas piping, venting and combustion air. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department about permits. Verify any installer on the Department of Labor and Industries Verify page.

Recirculation, flush and service for a big tankless unit

A high-output tankless heater delivers plenty of hot water but does not make it arrive faster. The wait at a distant tap depends on the pipe between the heater and the fixture. A recirculation pump or a dedicated return line can shorten the wait, and many tankless models support one. Ask your plumber what your unit allows.

Maintenance matters more as the heat exchanger works harder. Descaling flushes the exchanger with a mild acid solution, and most makers recommend it on a regular schedule, with more frequent service in harder water. Clean the inlet filter, check the vent for blockage and keep the area around the unit clear.

Pay attention to error codes. Most units show a code for ignition trouble, vent blockage or flow problems. Write down the code and call before you reset it repeatedly. Repeated resets can mask a real fault that needs attention.

Is 200,000 BTU the right size for your home?

A smaller home with one or two bathrooms may be fine with a smaller unit, and a very large home may want more capacity or two units. The right size depends on your peak demand, your winter inlet temperature and your gas supply. Oversizing costs more up front and may need a larger gas line.

Questions we hear

How many gpm does a 200,000 BTU tankless produce?

Roughly 5 to 8 gpm depending on the temperature rise. Use output BTU divided by 500 times the rise, and check the manufacturer's chart.

Can it run two showers at once?

Usually yes in mild water temperatures. In winter, flow drops, so confirm with the numbers.

Does a 200,000 BTU unit need a bigger gas line?

Often yes. Confirm the gas line size and the meter capacity with your gas utility and plumber.

Does hardness matter for a tankless heater?

Scale can build in the heat exchanger over time. Snohomish County PUD lists 9 to 89 ppm for Lake Stevens, so plan a yearly flush.

Do I need a permit?

Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department. Gas and venting work is usually inspected.
