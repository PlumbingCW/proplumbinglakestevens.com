Verify any plumber in Washington

Washington's Department of Labor and Industries licenses contractors and certifies plumbers. Its Verify page lets you look up a contractor's license, any safety citations and whether a plumber is certified. Use it for any plumber, including us.

We do not list license numbers or named technicians on this site because the plumber who arrives depends on the day and the job. Ask the plumber at the door for their certification, and check it on the Verify page if you like.

Standards we point you to

We do not claim credentials we cannot show you. Instead, we point you to public standards and tools you can check yourself. Washington follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments under WAC 51-56, and the Department of Labor and Industries Verify page shows license and certification status for plumbers and contractors.

How pricing and quotes work

You hear a price before work starts. For a repair, the plumber diagnoses the problem, explains what is wrong and quotes the fix. For a replacement, such as a water heater, the quote covers the unit, the installation work and hauling away the old one. If the plumber finds something unexpected once a wall or a floor is open, the price is discussed again before extra work begins.

Several things move a plumbing price. Access matters, since a pipe in an open crawlspace costs less to reach than one behind tile. Materials matter, because copper, PEX and cast iron repairs differ. Time of day matters for emergency calls. The size of the job matters, and so does whether the plumber must bring special equipment such as a drain camera or a hydro jetter. Ask what is included, and ask what would change the number.

If you have a question about a price, call (360) 371-6092 before you agree to the work. Our job is to make sure you understand the quote.

What to expect when the plumber arrives

A plumber should introduce themselves, ask about the problem and look at it before touching anything. They should put down floor protection when they work indoors, explain what they found in plain words and show you the failed part when that is possible. They should test the repair before they leave and clean the work area.

You can help by clearing a path to the work area, showing the plumber where the main water shutoff is and putting pets in another room. If the problem is a leak, tell the plumber what you already shut off and when the problem started. If you took photos, show them.

After the visit, keep your paperwork. Many repairs and installs come with parts or labor warranties, and the paperwork is how you use them. Read the terms when you get them and ask for clarification if anything is unclear.
