A jammed or leaking disposal needs quick attention

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. A disposal that leaks from the bottom is failing. Either one makes the kitchen sink hard to use, and a leak can ruin the cabinet floor. Our 24/7 line takes these calls.

While you wait, turn the disposal off at the wall switch and do not put your hand inside. If water is leaking, put a bowl under the unit and close the dishwasher connection if it is draining into the disposal.

Disposal symptoms and what they mean

  • Humming with no grinding: a jam, often from bones, fruit pits or a spoon.
  • Silent, no hum: a tripped reset button or a failed motor.
  • Leak from the bottom: failed seals, so replace the unit.
  • Leak from the top flange or side: a loose mount or a bad drain connection, which we can reseal.
  • Slow drain or backup: a clog past the disposal in the trap or drain line.
  • Foul smell: food buildup on the grinding chamber, which we can clean.

Repair or replace

Jams and tripped resets are quick fixes. We free the flywheel from below with an Allen wrench, reset the unit and test it. If the motor is burned out or the housing leaks, replacement is the better value because disposals are inexpensive compared to labor.

A leak is the most common reason we replace a disposal. Once the seals fail, the unit leaks from the lower housing and repair is not practical.

Disposals and Lake Stevens kitchens

The Lake Stevens Sewer District serves homes here, and many homes added since 2000 have plastic kitchen drains. Plastic lines do not corrode, but grease from a disposal can still harden inside them, so run cold water while grinding and avoid pouring fat down the drain.

Ask your local waste hauler or the city whether food waste is collected for composting in your area. Putting less in the disposal reduces clogs and wear.

Code and standards for disposals

A disposal connects to the sink drain under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted with state amendments in WAC 51-56, and to the home's electrical system under electrical rules. The unit needs a proper trap and an air gap or high loop for any dishwasher drain. Hardwiring or adding an outlet is electrical work for a licensed electrician.

Keeping a disposal running

Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after each use so the waste clears the trap. Feed small amounts at a time. Skip fibrous items such as celery and corn husks, grease, coffee grounds in large amounts and bones. A few citrus peels or ice cubes now and then are fine, and they help freshen the chamber.

If the unit hums but does not spin, switch it off, then use the hex socket under the unit to turn it by hand. Press the reset button on the bottom after it cools. Never put your hand into the chamber.

Questions we hear

Do you handle garbage disposal emergencies at night or on weekends in Lake Stevens?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7. Turn the disposal off at the wall and call us.

Why does my disposal hum but not spin?

It is jammed. We free the flywheel and reset it, which fixes most jams.

How long does a disposal last?

Many last about eight to fifteen years depending on use and what goes down them.

Can I install a disposal myself?

It involves plumbing and electrical connections. A mistake can leak or short out, so many homeowners hire a plumber.

What should I never put in a disposal?

Grease, bones, fibrous vegetables like celery and coffee grounds in large amounts.
