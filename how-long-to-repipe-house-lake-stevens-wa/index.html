How Long Does It Take to Repipe a House in Lake Stevens?

Most whole-house repipes take several days, with water off for part of each day. The size of the house, the pipe material and how easy it is to reach the old lines decide the schedule.

By the Lake Stevens Pro Plumbing team / Published November 13, 2026

A typical repipe timeline

A whole-house repipe for an average single-family home usually takes about two to five days. A small home with open access may finish sooner, and a large two-story house with finished walls may take longer. Partial repipes, such as a single bathroom wing, can finish in a day or two.

The schedule matters most for water. Plumbers typically shut off water while they cut in new lines and reconnect fixtures, and they restore service at the end of each day so you can cook and shower. Ask your plumber for the daily shutoff windows in writing before the job starts.

How the days usually unfold

  1. Day 1: plan and open accessWalk the house, mark fixtures and open small holes in drywall where lines will run.
  2. Day 2: run the new linesInstall PEX or copper from the main manifold to each fixture.
  3. Day 3: connect and testReconnect fixtures and water heater, then pressure test every line.
  4. Final day: close upRestore water, patch access holes and review the work with you.

What speeds up or slows down a repipe

FactorFasterSlower
AccessOpen crawlspace or basementSlab foundation or finished ceilings
MaterialPEX, which bends around cornersCopper, which needs soldering or press fittings
House sizeOne story, few bathroomsMultiple stories and many fixtures
ConditionStraightforward pipe runsHidden surprises, such as other damage

A repipe is the right choice only after a plumber confirms the lines are failing. Our leak detection service can confirm whether you have one repairable leak or a system-wide problem.

How to prepare your home

  • Move furniture away from walls where plumbers will cut access holes.
  • Store drinking water for the shutoff windows.
  • Plan for pets and children away from the work areas.
  • Clear a path to the water heater and the main shutoff.
  • Take photos of walls and ceilings before work starts.
Local note

Repiping in Lake Stevens homes

Lake Stevens incorporated in 1960 and went from 6,361 residents in 2000 to 28,069 in 2010. A house from that growth period likely has PEX or copper supply lines and rarely needs a repipe. Homes from the 1960s and 1970s are more likely to have older copper or galvanized steel nearing the end of its service life.

Snohomish County PUD lists total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm for Lake Stevens water, averaging 73. Water in that range is not the harshest on pipes, but it can still corrode or deposit minerals over decades. Ask the PUD for its latest report, or test your tap, if you want exact numbers.

Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department whether a repipe needs a permit and inspection. Verify any plumber's certification on the Department of Labor and Industries Verify page.

Questions to ask before you sign a repipe contract

Ask for the schedule in writing, including how many days water will be off and for how many hours each day. Ask who patches the drywall and who paints. Some plumbers include patching, and many leave finish work to the homeowner or another trade, and the difference changes both the price and the timeline.

Ask what pipe material will be used, what warranty comes with the work and how the lines will be supported and protected where they pass through framing. Ask whether the water heater connections and shutoff valves are included. Ask what happens if the plumber finds a hidden problem once walls are open, and how any extra work will be priced before it starts.

A clear written scope helps both sides. It avoids arguments about what was promised, and it gives you a checklist for the final walkthrough.

Living in the house during a repipe

Most families stay home. The main disruption is the water schedule and some dust from small access holes. Plumbers usually cover floors, work room by room and clean up daily. Ask the plumber where each shutoff window falls so you can plan showers, laundry and cooking.

If you have a baby, a home business or anyone who needs reliable water, tell the plumber ahead of time. A partial repipe or a staged schedule can reduce the disruption. See plumbing installation for related remodeling work.

Questions we hear

How long does a whole-house repipe take?

Most take about two to five days depending on house size and access. Your plumber should give you a schedule and daily shutoff windows.

Will I have water at night during a repipe?

Usually yes. Plumbers restore service at the end of each day, but confirm the schedule beforehand.

Is PEX faster than copper?

Generally yes. PEX bends and needs fewer fittings, so it speeds the work.

Do I have to move out?

Usually not. Many families stay home through a repipe.

Do I need a permit to repipe in Lake Stevens?

Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department. Permit rules vary by project.
