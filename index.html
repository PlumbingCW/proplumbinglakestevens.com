What Lake Stevens homes ask of a plumber

Lake Stevens incorporated in 1960 after residents voted to keep downtown businesses from moving to a new shopping center. It was a lake resort, then a commuter town, then one of the fastest-growing cities in the state, going from 6,361 residents in 2000 to 28,069 in 2010. A 2009 annexation in the southwest added more than 10,000 people.

That history shapes the plumbing. Older homes near downtown and the lake from the 1960s and 1970s can have galvanized or cast iron pipe that is reaching the end of its life. Homes from the 2000s growth years usually have PEX supply lines and plastic drains, where problems tend to come from fittings, clogs and water heaters.

Lake Stevens water comes through Snohomish County PUD, which buys most of it from Everett's Spada Lake supply and adds water from its own wells. The PUD's water quality report lists total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm, averaging 73, so scale builds slowly here compared with hard-water regions. Sewer service comes from the Lake Stevens Sewer District.

Plumbing problems we get called for most

These are the situations that bring the most calls to the 24/7 line.

  • Hidden leaks and high water bills. A meter that moves with every tap off is the clearest sign. See leak detection.

  • No hot water or a leaking tank. Gas, electric and tankless units. See water heater repair.
  • Sewer backups. Whole-house slow drains and sewage smells. See sewer line cleaning.
  • Toilets, faucets and disposals. Overflows, running toilets and jammed disposals.

Clear pricing and honest repair advice

You hear the price before work starts. For emergency calls that means a quote for the repair we recommend, not an open-ended hourly clock. If a repair is not worth it, we say so, and we explain what a replacement would involve.

Plumbing work in Washington is regulated. The state Department of Labor and Industries licenses contractors and certifies plumbers, and its Verify page lets you check either one by name or number. It is worth checking any plumber you hire.

Code, licensing and permits

Plumbing in Washington follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments, WAC 51-56. The City of Lake Stevens building department can tell you whether a repair needs a permit, and the Department of Labor and Industries Verify page lets you check a plumber's certification or a contractor's license before you agree to work.
