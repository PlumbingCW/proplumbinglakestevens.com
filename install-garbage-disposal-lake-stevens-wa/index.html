How Hard Is It to Install a Garbage Disposal? A Lake Stevens Guide

Swapping an existing disposal is a manageable project. Adding one where none existed takes wiring, a switch and drain work. Here is what each version involves and where homeowners get stuck.

Call (360) 371-6092

By the Lake Stevens Pro Plumbing team / Published November 13, 2026

Replacement versus a brand-new installation

If a disposal is already in your sink, replacing it is mostly a mechanical job. Turn off power, disconnect the drain and dishwasher line, loosen the mounting ring, lift out the old unit and mount the new one. Most homeowners with basic tools can do it in an hour or two.

A new installation is harder. The sink flange must be fitted, a drain connection set, power wired and a switch added. In many kitchens, no outlet sits under the sink, so an electrician or a plumber with electrical skills must add one. That step is the usual reason to call a pro.

The installation steps in order

  1. Cut powerSwitch off the breaker and confirm the circuit is dead.
  2. Remove the old unitDisconnect the drain, dishwasher hose and mounting ring.
  3. Mount the new flangeSeat the sink flange with plumber's putty and secure the mounting assembly.
  4. Connect and testAttach the drain, dishwasher hose and power, then run water and grind.

A leak under the sink is more than a nuisance because it can rot the cabinet floor. If you find water, see our garbage disposal repair page.

Before you buy a disposal

  • Check the sink: most stainless sinks fit a standard three-bolt mount, and some sinks need a special flange.
  • Check the power: a switched outlet or an electrician-installed circuit.
  • Check the horsepower: one-third to three-quarter horsepower suits most homes.
  • Check the dishwasher connection: a knockout plug must be removed.
  • Check cabinet space: the unit and drain must fit with a trap below.

What Lake Stevens kitchens need to know

Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments. Kitchen sink drains need a properly vented trap and arm, and the disposal must connect to that drain with approved fittings. Power, switch and dishwasher connections

A disposal needs power, and the way it is wired matters. Many kitchens already have a switched outlet under the sink for this purpose. If yours does not, a licensed electrician should add one rather than leave a cord running to an unrelated outlet. A switch within reach of the sink is both convenient and a safety feature.

If a dishwasher drains through the disposal, the dishwasher hose connects to a small inlet on the disposal body. A new unit has a knockout plug in that inlet. Forgetting to remove the plug is a classic mistake, and it leaves the dishwasher draining slowly into the sink or backing up. Remove the plug with a screwdriver and a light tap before the dishwasher hose goes on.

Finish by running cold water, switching the unit on and watching every joint for drips with a dry paper towel. Check again after a day, since small leaks sometimes show only after the sink holds a full load of water.

Should you hire a plumber?

Hire a plumber if the sink has no outlet, if the drain is corroded or misaligned, or if you are not comfortable working under a sink. A leak left unnoticed can damage cabinets in a few days.

Questions we hear

How long does it take to install a garbage disposal?

A replacement usually takes one to two hours. A first-time install with new wiring takes longer.

Do I need an electrician to install a disposal?

If an outlet and switch are not already present, yes, or a plumber who also handles the electrical connection.

Can a disposal be installed in any sink?

Most sinks accept a standard mount, but some need special flanges. Check the manufacturer's guide.

Do I need a permit in Lake Stevens?

Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department. Permit rules vary by project.

What should I never put in a disposal?

Grease, bones, fibrous vegetables and large amounts of coffee grounds. They can clog the drain line.
