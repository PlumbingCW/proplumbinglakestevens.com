What is verified about Downtown Lake Stevens

The downtown area grew around North Cove, the outlet at the north end of the lake. Charles Missimer signed a deed for lots there on October 8, 1889, and a two-block townsite called Outing was platted in 1890. The Rucker Brothers sawmill opened in 1907, employed about 250 people by 1910 and burned in 1925.

The City of Lake Stevens adopted its Downtown Lake Stevens Subarea Plan on July 10, 2018. The plan covers roughly 200 acres, including 103 single-family parcels and a number of multifamily properties, and the city describes this as the oldest part of Lake Stevens. The plan also looks at water and sewer improvements for the district.

What this means for you: the area mixes older lots with newer buildings, so you cannot assume what is under your floor from the neighborhood name alone. A plumber has to look at your own pipes.

What we do not know, and how we handle it

We do not have a record of the pipe material or the building age at your address, and we will not guess. Some homes downtown may have older drain lines and some may have been updated, and only an inspection tells you which.

If you have a drain that keeps slowing down, a sewer camera inspection shows the real condition of the line. A camera also helps if you plan to buy, sell or remodel a property here.

Local note

Water, sewer and permits near North Cove

Water in the Lake Stevens area is supplied by Snohomish County PUD, whose water line is 425-397-3000. Sewer service comes from the Lake Stevens Sewer District. If water is bubbling near the street or the meter, the utility may be responsible, and if the problem is inside your property line, it is yours.

Construction in the downtown subarea falls under city rules. Ask the City of Lake Stevens whether a repair or a remodel needs a permit before you start. You can check any plumber's certification on the Washington Department of Labor and Industries Verify page.
