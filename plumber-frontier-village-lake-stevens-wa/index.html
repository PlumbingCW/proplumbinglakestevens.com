What is verified about the Frontier Village area

The Frontier Village shopping center opened in 1960 at the crossing of State Route 9 and State Route 204 on the west side of the lake. It drew businesses away from old downtown, and the town incorporated later that year.

In 2006 the Daily Herald reported a 708-acre annexation on the west side of the lake that would add about 3,700 people, including businesses along Highway 9. The city's own annexation history lists 2006 as an annexation year. We have not mapped the exact boundary, so call with your address and we will confirm that we cover it.

What this means for you: the area has both retail property and homes, and the plumbing needs are different, so we describe them separately below.

For homes near Frontier Village

Homes on the west side vary, and we do not claim a typical age or pipe material. Common calls for any house are slow drains, running toilets, faucet leaks and water heaters. See drain cleaning and water heater repair for what each visit involves.

If you see water where it should not be, shut off the main valve first. Then call, and tell us which fixtures are affected. A burst pipe needs a faster response than a dripping faucet, and we say so plainly.

For shops and offices along SR 9 and SR 204

Retail and office buildings have their own plumbing questions. Restaurant and shop drains collect grease and debris, so a regular hydro jetting visit can keep a line open. Shared walls and tenant spaces also raise the question of who owns a problem, which is a matter for your lease and landlord.

Our commercial plumbing page explains what we handle for businesses. We work around your hours when we can, and we explain the price before work starts.

Local note

Water, sewer and licensing

Snohomish County PUD supplies water in the Lake Stevens area, and its water line is 425-397-3000. The Lake Stevens Sewer District handles sewer service. If you are not sure whether a leak is on the utility side of the meter, call the utility, and call us if it is inside your property.

Check any plumber or contractor on the Washington Department of Labor and Industries Verify page. Your landlord or the property manager is the first contact for plumbing in a rented space.
