Downtown Marysville and the Ebey Slough side: drains and backups

Marysville's downtown sits on delta land that is about 5 feet above sea level, with Ebey Slough forming the southern boundary. Low ground leaves little margin when rain saturates the soil, so drains and basement fixtures are more prone to slow-line problems in heavy rain. A floor drain that weeps during a storm is a sign the line is slow, not just wet weather.

The streets around downtown are among the older parts of the city. Marysville was founded in 1872 and incorporated in 1891, and the water tower in Comeford Park dates to 1921. Houses of that age are more likely to have older drain pipe, so repeat clogs deserve a sewer camera inspection before another cleaning.

If a backup is in progress, stop running water and call. Our sewer line cleaning crew clears the main, and we camera the pipe to find why it plugged.

North Marysville, Smokey Point and newer neighborhoods: water heaters and fixtures

The 2009 annexation of North Marysville brought about 20,000 residents into the city, and the city has grown north toward Smokey Point since 2000. Homes from this wave generally have plastic drains and PEX or copper supply, so the common calls are water heaters, fixtures and clogs. See water heater repair for no-hot-water calls.

A water heater in a newer home often fails from sediment or a leak at the relief valve, not from rusted-out fittings. If the tank itself is leaking, our emergency water heater replacement crew sets a new unit and hauls away the old one. A clear price comes first.

Water, sewer and licensing in Marysville

Marysville buys water from its own utility. The city's water quality report lists several sources, including the Lake Goodwin well, a Ranney well on the Stillaguamish River, the Sunnyside treatment facility, Edward Springs and water purchased from the City of Everett, which draws from Spada Lake. The report does not give a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask Public Works. The Water Division number is (360) 363-8100.

The Lake Stevens School District reaches into southeast Marysville, which gives you a sense of how closely the two cities overlap. Wastewater inside Marysville goes to the city's system, so ask the city where its line ends and your private side sewer begins before you authorize work.

Plumbers in Washington are certified by the Department of Labor and Industries, and you can check a plumber or a contractor on its Verify page. Marysville's building department can tell you if a repair needs a permit.
