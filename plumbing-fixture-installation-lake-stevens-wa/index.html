Plumbing Fixture Installation in Lake Stevens, WA

A fixture swap looks simple until a corroded shutoff valve snaps or the new faucet does not fit the old holes. Knowing what to check first keeps a one-hour job from becoming a flood.

By the Lake Stevens Pro Plumbing team / Published November 13, 2026

Check before you buy the fixture

Start with the measurements. A faucet needs the right number of holes in the sink or counter, usually one, two or three. A toilet needs the right rough-in distance from the wall to the center of the drain, commonly 12 inches but not always. A vanity sink needs a drain that lines up with the trap.

Next, check the shutoff valves under the fixture. Old valves often seize or leak when turned. If a valve will not close fully, the fixture swap needs a valve replacement first, and that may mean shutting off water to the whole house.

Before you start the swap

  • Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the house main.
  • Open the faucet to drain the line and release pressure.
  • Put a bucket and towels under the work area.
  • Photograph the old connections before you disconnect them.
  • Test for leaks with paper towel at each joint after turning water on.

Supply lines, seals and thread tape

Replace flexible supply lines when you replace a fixture. Old lines can crack or corrode. Hand-tighten, then give a small turn with a wrench. Over-tightening damages washers and causes the leaks you are trying to prevent. Use thread seal tape on threaded pipe connections that need it, and leave it off compression fittings that seal with a washer or ferrule.

Always run water for a few minutes after the swap and check every joint with a dry paper towel. A slow drip left alone can damage a vanity or subfloor quickly.

Local note

Fixture notes for Lake Stevens homes

Snohomish County PUD supplies Lake Stevens water at a total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm, averaging 73. That range leaves modest mineral buildup, but aerators and showerheads still collect deposits. Clean or replace them if flow drops.

Lake Stevens went from 6,361 residents in 2000 to 28,069 in 2010, so many homes have fixtures from the 2000s that are now reaching replacement age. Homes from the 1960s and 1970s may have older shutoff valves that are more likely to seize.

Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments. Many simple like-for-like swaps do not need a permit, but moving a fixture or changing a drain does. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department if you are unsure.

Sealing, caulking and finishing details

The finishing details decide how a fixture looks and how long it lasts. A bead of silicone caulk where a sink meets the counter keeps water from seeping into the cabinet below. A thin ring of plumber's putty under a drain flange makes a seal that is easy to service later. Wipe away excess right away so the finished surface looks clean.

Use the right sealant for each place. Silicone suits wet areas and flexible joints. Plumber's putty suits metal drain parts that may be removed again. Thread tape suits tapered threads, while a washer or rubber gasket does the sealing on compression and hose fittings. Using the wrong one is a common cause of slow leaks.

Before you leave the job, check that every handle moves smoothly, every aerator is seated and every valve opens and closes fully. Run both hot and cold, flush the toilet twice and fill the sink to test the overflow. A ten minute test is worth a call back.

When to hire a plumber for fixtures

Call a plumber if the shutoff valve fails, if pipe behind the wall must be cut, if the drain is slow after the swap or if you are moving a fixture to a new spot. A pro also brings the right tools for tight sink bases and stubborn nuts.

If a fixture leaks after installation, turn off the water and call. For active leaks that threaten floors, see burst pipe repair. For slow drains, see drain cleaning. We also serve Marysville and nearby towns. See our Marysville emergency plumber page and the service area page.

Questions we hear

Can I replace a faucet myself?

Often yes, if the shutoff valves work and you have access under the sink. Call a plumber if valves seize or pipe is behind a wall.

What is toilet rough-in?

It is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the drain, commonly 12 inches. Measure before you buy a toilet.

Should I replace supply lines with a new fixture?

Yes. Old lines can crack or corrode, and a new line is cheap insurance.

Do I need a permit to replace a fixture in Lake Stevens?

Like-for-like swaps often do not. Moving a fixture or changing drains usually does. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department.

Why does my new faucet drip?

Common causes are an overtightened or misseated washer, a loose connection or debris in the cartridge.
