Plumbing installation for homes and remodels

A plumbing installation covers new fixtures, new lines and the rough-in behind walls. It is different from repair because it starts with a plan. Where will the drain go, how will the vent run, and does the supply line have enough capacity?

We handle bathroom additions, kitchen remodels, basement baths, laundry rooms and outdoor lines. We coordinate with your electrician and contractor on timing so walls are not closed before the inspection.

Installations we do

  • Bathroom plumbing: toilets, sinks, tubs, shower valves and drains.
  • Kitchen plumbing: sinks, faucets, dishwashers and disposals.
  • Laundry and utility: washer boxes, utility sinks and drain lines.
  • Outdoor lines: hose bibs, outdoor kitchens and irrigation connections.
  • New construction rough-in: supply and drain layout for new builds.
  • Fixture swaps: like-for-like or upgraded fixtures.

How to install plumbing for a bathroom

A bathroom needs three things: supply lines for hot and cold water, a drain for each fixture and a vent to let air in so drains flow. Rough-in happens before walls are closed. We run supply to the toilet, sink and shower, set drain pipes at the right slope and connect vents to the stack.

Outdoor plumbing has its own rules. A frost-proof hose bib extends into the heated house so the valve stays warm. For an outdoor kitchen, we plan supply lines, a drain and a shutoff you can close for cold snaps.

Permits and code in Lake Stevens

Washington's state plumbing code adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments. New plumbing is normally inspected before walls are closed. Because permit rules vary by project, ask the City of Lake Stevens building department what your plans need.

A licensed plumber is the safer choice for new work. You can verify any plumber's certification and any contractor's license on the Washington Department of Labor and Industries Verify page.

Code and standards for new installs

New plumbing follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted in Washington with state amendments, WAC 51-56. The code sets drain sizes, vent rules and fixture clearances, and inspectors check them before walls close. Plan permits and inspections into the schedule from the start.

Planning a plumbing installation

Good installs begin with a plan. Walk the project with the plumber and mark where each fixture goes. Confirm drain locations, vent paths and the supply route. Ask about access panels so the next repair does not need a wall opened. Check that your water heater and supply pipe can handle the added demand.

Ask about permits and inspections at the start. Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments, and the City of Lake Stevens building department can tell you what your project needs. Inspections usually happen before walls close, so the schedule matters.

Choose fixtures before the rough-in. Rough-in dimensions depend on the exact toilet, sink, tub and valve, and changing the fixture after the pipes are set can force rework. Keep the specification sheets and give them to the plumber.

Questions we hear

How long does it take to install plumbing for a bathroom?

Rough-in for a typical bathroom takes a day or two, and fixtures are set after finishes are in. Scheduling with other trades affects the timeline.

Can you install an outdoor spigot?

Yes. We install frost-proof hose bibs and outdoor lines with shutoffs that suit cold weather.

Do I need a permit for new plumbing?

New fixtures and lines are often inspected. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department about your project.

Do you install fixtures I buy myself?

Yes, though bringing the model number ahead of time helps us check compatibility.

Can you work with my contractor?

Yes. We coordinate timing so inspections and finishes go smoothly.
