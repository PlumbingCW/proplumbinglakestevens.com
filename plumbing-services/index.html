Emergency work versus planned work

Emergency work starts with stopping damage. We shut off water, clear blockages and make temporary repairs, then talk through a lasting fix. Planned work starts with a plan. We measure, choose parts and schedule around your life.

Many problems start as one and become the other. A slow drain is a planned drain cleaning visit today and a sewer line cleaning emergency if you wait. A leak you cannot find is a leak detection job before it becomes a burst pipe repair.

Standards that apply across our services

Washington follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments, published as WAC 51-56, and most of the work described on these pages falls under it. Where a manufacturer's installation manual is stricter, the plumber follows the manual. Permit rules differ by city, so ask the City of Lake Stevens building department about any project that changes pipe, fixtures or gas connections.

How to choose between a repair and a replacement

Many plumbing calls end with the same question: fix it or replace it? A good plumber explains both. Repairs make sense when the part is cheap, the rest of the system is sound and the failure was a one-off. Replacement makes sense when the same part keeps failing, when the equipment is near the end of its expected life or when repair costs approach the price of something new.

Age and condition matter. A faucet with a worn cartridge is a simple repair, while a faucet with a corroded body is a replacement. A water heater with a bad thermostat may be worth repairing, and a tank that is leaking from the shell is not. A sewer line with one cracked joint may be spot repaired, while a pipe that is cracked along its length needs more.

Ask your plumber to give you both options with prices when the choice is close, or call (360) 371-6092 to talk it through. That lets you decide with the facts, rather than relying on a hunch.

What every service visit should include

No matter which service you need, a good visit has the same shape. The plumber listens to your description, examines the problem and explains what they found. They give a price before they start, do the work, test it and clean up. They tell you what to watch for afterward and give you the paperwork.

If a visit does not look like that, ask questions. You can call (360) 371-6092 any hour to talk through a quote or a concern. You can also check any plumber or contractor on the Washington Department of Labor and Industries Verify page, which shows licensing and certification status.

Local conditions that shape plumbing here

Lake Stevens water comes from Snohomish County PUD, with total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm and an average of 73. Scale is modest, but heaters and fixtures still benefit from yearly maintenance. Sewer service comes from the Lake Stevens Sewer District, and the city grew from 6,361 residents in 2000 to 28,069 in 2010, so the housing stock mixes older pipe near the lake and downtown with newer plastic systems in newer neighborhoods.

That mix means two common patterns. Older homes call for drain cleaning, sewer inspection and repiping questions. Newer homes call for water heater, fixture and disposal work. Both benefit from leak detection when a bill climbs unexpectedly.
