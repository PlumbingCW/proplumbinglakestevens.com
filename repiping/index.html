Repiping in Lake Stevens, WA

Repeated pinhole leaks, rusty water and low pressure are signs that supply pipes are wearing out. We replace failing lines with PEX or copper, one section or the whole house.

When repiping makes sense

A single leak does not mean repiping. A pinhole leak in a copper line can be repaired. When pinholes keep appearing in different spots, the pipe is failing from the inside and repairs will not keep up. Rusty water at every tap and a long-term drop in pressure point the same way.

Repiping replaces the supply lines that bring water to fixtures. It does not usually include drains. We start with an assessment to see whether a partial repipe on one trouble area is enough or a whole-house job is warranted.

Signs your supply pipes are wearing out

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in copper or galvanized lines.
  • Rust-colored water, especially first thing in the morning.
  • Low pressure at several fixtures, even with a good main.
  • Visible corrosion on pipes in the crawlspace or basement.
  • Pipes of a known problem material such as galvanized steel or polybutylene.

Galvanized pipe, low pressure and water service lines

Galvanized steel was common in homes decades ago. Zinc coating wears away, and the inside of the pipe fills with rust and scale. Pressure drops, water turns brown and joints begin to leak. If your house has galvanized lines, replacement is a matter of when, not if.

The water service line, which runs from the meter to the house, is separate from the pipes inside. A leak or heavy corrosion in that line can cause low pressure and high bills. We locate the problem and discuss replacement options.

How a repipe is done

  1. Plan the routeWe map where each fixture sits and choose the shortest path.
  2. Open small access holesWe cut limited openings in drywall and run new lines through them.
  3. Install and testNew lines are connected and pressure tested before they are covered.
  4. Patch and restoreWe close access holes and leave the house ready for finish repairs.

Code and standards for a repipe

A repipe follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted in Washington with state amendments under WAC 51-56, which governs approved pipe materials, supports and pressure testing. Whole-house repipes are commonly permitted and inspected. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department what yours needs, and verify any plumber on the Department of Labor and Industries Verify page.

Questions we hear

How long does repiping take?

A typical whole-house repipe takes several days, depending on size and access. We tell you the water shutoff windows in advance.

Do I have to leave the house during a repipe?

Usually not, though water will be off for part of each day.

Will repiping damage my walls?

We cut small access holes and patch them. Finish painting is separate.

Is PEX or copper better?

Both work. PEX is flexible and quicker to install. Copper is rigid and has a long track record.

Do I need a permit to repipe?

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Lake Stevens building department what applies. We explain the scope so you can ask precisely.
