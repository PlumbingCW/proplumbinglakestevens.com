Replacing a Gas Water Heater in Lake Stevens, WA

A gas tank swap is a half-day job when the vent, gas line and drain are in good shape. Here is how to plan it, what the plumber checks and where Lake Stevens homes tend to hit snags.

Call (360) 371-6092

By the Lake Stevens Pro Plumbing team / Published November 13, 2026

The short answer: plan for sizing, venting and the gas connection

Replacing a gas water heater means draining the old tank, disconnecting the gas line, vent and water lines, setting a new tank and reconnecting everything with new fittings and a tested vent. In a house where nothing else changes, it usually takes a half day. The risk sits in the details: the vent path, the gas shutoff and the relief valve discharge.

Before you buy a tank, write down the capacity on the label of your old unit, note whether it vents through a roof flue or a side wall and find the gas shutoff on the line. Those three facts decide what fits. A plumber confirms the rest when they see the room.

What a proper gas replacement includes

  • New flexible gas connector and a sediment trap at the gas supply, tested for leaks.
  • Vent connection checked for slope, sealing and clearance, and tested for draft.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that ends in a safe place.
  • New water connectors and shutoff valves with an expansion tank where the system calls for one.
  • Seismic strapping where code requires it, and a drain pan where the room needs one.

Sizing the new tank

Tank size should follow your morning rush, not your floor plan. A 40-gallon tank often covers two or three people, and a 50-gallon tank suits many four-person homes. A household with a soaking tub or several teenagers might step up to 60 or 75 gallons.

Gas tanks recover quickly, so a slightly smaller tank often works than an electric one would. If your current tank never ran out of hot water, a same-size replacement is the safe choice. If it ran short, a larger tank or a tankless unit is worth comparing. Our tankless water heater page explains the trade-offs and gas line limits.

Local note

Lake Stevens notes: water, code and permits

Snohomish County PUD supplies Lake Stevens water, and its published water quality report lists total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm with an average of 73. For comparison, a grain per gallon is roughly 17 ppm, so the average works out to a little over four grains. That is moderate, so a yearly tank flush and an anode rod check are worth doing, though extreme scale is not typical here.

Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments, and replacements that change gas piping or venting are inspected. Permit rules vary by project, so ask the City of Lake Stevens building department what yours needs. You can also verify any plumber's certification on the state Department of Labor and Industries Verify page.

Lake Stevens grew from 6,361 residents in 2000 to 28,069 in 2010, so many of its homes have garage or closet heaters with straightforward access. Older homes from the 1960s and 1970s sometimes have heaters in crawlspaces or tight utility closets, which is where a plumber's access check matters most.

A step-by-step view of the swap

Plumbers shut off gas and water first, then drain the tank to a floor drain or outside. They disconnect the vent, gas line and water lines and lift out the old unit. The new tank is leveled on a stand or pan, connected to water and gas, vented and strapped.

Next comes the leak check. A soapy-water test at each gas joint, a pressure check on the water lines and a draft test at the vent confirm that the system is safe. Then the pilot is lit or the electronic ignition is set, the thermostat is adjusted and the plumber shows you the shutoffs.

Questions we hear

How long does replacing a gas water heater take?

A like-for-like swap in an accessible spot usually takes a half day. Vent repairs, a new gas line or a move to a new location can stretch it to a full day.

Can I replace a gas water heater myself?

Gas and venting work affects safety. A leak or a bad vent can cause fire or carbon monoxide exposure, so most homeowners hire a certified plumber.

Do I need a permit in Lake Stevens?

Permit rules vary by project, so ask the City of Lake Stevens building department what applies to your replacement.

Is Lake Stevens water hard enough to hurt a tank?

Snohomish County PUD lists hardness from 9 to 89 ppm for its Lake Stevens system, averaging 73. That is a modest range, and a yearly flush plus an anode rod check help the tank last.

What should I do if I smell gas near the heater?

Leave the house, avoid switches and phones inside, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Call a plumber after the utility clears the house.
