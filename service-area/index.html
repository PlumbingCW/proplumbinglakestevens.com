Permits and codes in nearby towns

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with Washington's state amendments, WAC 51-56, applies statewide, but permits and inspections are handled by each city or by the county. Ask the building department in your town what a project needs, and give dispatch your address so we know which one applies.

Water and sewer providers differ by town

Before you call a plumber about a water or sewer problem, it helps to know who supplies your service. In Lake Stevens, water comes from Snohomish County PUD, whose water utility line is 425-397-3000, and sewer service comes from the Lake Stevens Sewer District. In Marysville, the City of Marysville Public Works Water Division, at (360) 363-8100, runs the water utility, and the city handles wastewater.

Other towns have their own providers, and some rural addresses use wells and septic systems.

Why does that matter? A problem on the utility side of the meter or the property line is the utility's responsibility, and a problem on your side belongs to you. If you see water bubbling up near the street or the meter, call the utility. If the leak is inside your home or on your private line, call us. Our dispatch can help you sort out which is which.

What to have ready when you call

Dispatch moves faster when you have a few facts ready. Know your street address and town, and which ZIP code applies. Know where your main water shutoff is, and whether you have shut it off. Describe what you see, hear and smell. Tell us which fixtures are affected, how long it has been going on and whether anyone has tried to fix it.

If you are not sure whether your address is in our coverage area, call (360) 371-6092 and ask. Every call goes to the same 24/7 line, so call now if the problem is active. We confirm the address, tell you what to expect and give you shutoff steps while we arrange a plumber.

Septic systems, wells and side sewers

Not every home around Lake Stevens is on a public sewer. Some properties on the edges of the towns we serve use septic systems and private wells, which have different failure modes and different rules. A septic backup is not fixed by a sewer cable, and a well pump problem is not a plumbing leak. Tell dispatch if you have either one so we send the right help.

For homes on public sewer, the side sewer is the private pipe that runs from the house to the public main. It is usually the owner's responsibility. Tree roots, age and ground movement can all damage it, and a camera inspection shows what is happening. See sewer inspection for details.
