Sewer Camera Inspection in Lake Stevens, WA

A sewer camera shows the pipe from the inside, so you can stop guessing at roots, cracks and sags. We run a self-leveling camera through your line and show you what we find.

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A sewer camera is a waterproof lens on a flexible rod, with a light and a locator that tells us where the camera is underground. We feed it through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and follow the line toward the street. The monitor shows cracks, offset joints, root intrusion, grease buildup, bellies and any connections.

Most plumbers use a camera after clearing a clog to find out why it happened. It is just as useful before a clog. A planned inspection catches problems while they are small and cheap, and before they turn into a midnight backup.

When a camera inspection makes sense

  • A drain has backed up more than once in the same place.
  • You are buying an older house and want to know the condition of the sewer before closing.
  • A tree has grown near the line, or you see roots in a cleanout.
  • You plan to jet or line the pipe, and need to know it is sound first.
  • Soggy ground or smells appear in the yard without an obvious cause.

How an inspection runs

  1. Find accessWe locate the cleanout or pull a toilet if there is none.
  2. Run the cameraThe camera travels the line while we watch for defects.
  3. Locate and markWe trace the signal to the surface to mark any problem spot.
  4. Review the footageWe explain what we saw and what, if anything, to do next.

Reading the footage: what is normal and what is not

Some wear is normal. A little scale in cast iron or a hairline joint gap in old clay is not an emergency. Roots in a joint, a crushed or offset pipe, or a long belly that holds water are the findings that call for action. We tell you which category each finding falls into.

A belly is a low section where waste pools. It is easy to spot on camera because water stays in the pipe after flow stops. Cleaning a bellied line only helps for a while. The lasting fix is excavation and regrading that section.

Local note

Inspecting sewers in Lake Stevens homes

If you are buying a house here, a sewer inspection is a good use of a small fee. The city grew quickly after 2000 and again after the 2009 annexation southwest of the city, so many lines are newer and in good condition. Older streets around downtown and the lake date back further and are more likely to show roots or older materials.

The Lake Stevens Sewer District serves the area, and your side sewer is your responsibility up to a point the district can identify. If the camera shows damage near the street connection, ask the district who owns that section before you plan any repair.

Standards for a useful inspection

A sewer camera inspection should give you recorded video and a plain description of what was found, including where on the line it sits. The findings guide any repair, which then follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Washington under WAC 51-56. Keep the recording, since a buyer, an insurer or a second plumber may want to see it.

Questions we hear

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Usually about an hour for a standard house line. Long runs, heavy buildup or hard access can take longer.

Do I need a cleanout for a camera inspection?

It helps. Without one we can pull a toilet to get access, though a permanent cleanout is a worthwhile addition.

Can I keep the video?

Yes. We share the footage so you can review it or show it to an insurer or a seller.

Is a camera inspection worth it before buying a home?

Yes. A sewer repair can be one of the most expensive surprises after closing, and a camera catches defects in advance.

Do you also offer emergency camera inspections?

Yes. If a line is backing up, call the 24/7 line and we clear it first, then camera the pipe to find out why.
