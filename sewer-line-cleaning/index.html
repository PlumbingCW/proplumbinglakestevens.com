Main sewer backup: act now

A main sewer backup shows up at the lowest fixture first. That is often a basement floor drain, a ground-floor tub or a toilet. If you see wastewater rising, stop using water immediately and call our 24/7 line.

Every flush and every load of laundry sends more water into a plugged pipe. Stopping water use buys time and limits the damage. Keep people and pets out of the affected area, since sewage carries bacteria.

What clogs a main sewer line

Roots are a leading cause, entering at pipe joints and growing into a mat that catches paper and waste. Grease is the second, building along the pipe wall until the opening is the size of a pencil. Flushed wipes, paper towels and feminine products make up much of the rest.

Less common causes include a collapsed pipe, a belly that traps waste and a broken pipe where soil enters. A camera tells us which one we are dealing with, and we recommend one whenever the same clog returns.

Sewage in the house: first steps

Wastewater inside a home is a health issue. These steps limit exposure and help us work faster.

  1. Stop water. Shut off supply to fixtures that drain to the main if you can, or simply stop using them.
  2. Avoid contact. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash well afterward.
  3. Open windows. Ventilate the area if it is safe and dry underfoot.
  4. Call dispatch. Give us the lowest fixture that is backing up and how long it has been happening.

Code and standards in the cleaning

Cleanouts required by the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted in Washington with state amendments under WAC 51-56, are the usual access point for cleaning a main line. If your home lacks an accessible cleanout, a plumber may recommend adding one, which is work the building department can tell you how to permit.

Questions we hear

Do you handle sewer emergencies at night or on weekends in Lake Stevens?

Yes. Our dispatch line is staffed around the clock. Stop using water and call, and we will send equipment suited to a main-line blockage.

How do I tell if my main sewer line is blocked?

Several fixtures backing up together, or the lowest drain overflowing, points to a main-line clog. A single slow sink is usually a branch clog.

Will the sewer clog come back?

If roots or a belly caused it, yes, until the pipe is repaired. A camera will show the reason.

Can I clean the main line myself?

Rented equipment can injure you or the pipe, and chemical cleaners rarely reach a main-line clog. A plumber with the right cable and a camera is the safer route.

How often should a main sewer line be cleaned?

Only when the line shows symptoms or a camera finds buildup. Homes with big trees or a history of clogs may benefit from periodic checks.
