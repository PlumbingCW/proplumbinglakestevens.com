Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage backing up into a floor drain or tub is a health risk and an emergency. Stop using water, keep people and pets out of the affected area and call our 24/7 line. Do not run the washer or flush the toilet, because each gallon adds to the flood.

On arrival we clear the line enough to restore flow, then run a camera to find out why it failed. A line that clogs once may only need cleaning. A line that clogs repeatedly usually has a crack, a root intrusion or a sag that traps waste.

Signs of a damaged sewer line

  • Repeat backups in the same fixtures after cleaning.
  • Soggy or sunken ground above the sewer path in the yard.
  • Sewage smell outside near the cleanout or in the basement.
  • Gurgling drains when water runs elsewhere in the house.
  • Lush patches of lawn along the line, from leaking waste feeding the grass.
  • Cracks or offset joints seen on a camera inspection.

Repair options for a failed sewer line

The right repair depends on where the damage is, how long it runs and how deep the pipe sits.

We recommend the least invasive repair the camera supports. Our sewer camera inspection comes first so the repair plan is based on what is actually in the pipe.

Cast iron, clay and plastic: what each does when it fails

Cast iron rusts from the inside and flakes into rough scale that catches debris. Clay pipe cracks and lets roots enter at the joints. Plastic pipe, used in newer lines, rarely corrodes but can sag if the trench was not bedded well or shift at a joint.

Cast iron and orangeburg pipe from older homes may need a section replaced, and sometimes the whole run. We say what we see on camera and show you the footage before you decide.

Local note

Sewer lines and responsibility in Lake Stevens

Sewer service in Lake Stevens is provided by the Lake Stevens Sewer District. The City of Lake Stevens has been working to consolidate the district into its public works department, and that process has been tied up in litigation. For homeowners, the practical point is to ask the district where its line ends and your side sewer begins.

Much of Lake Stevens was built during the rapid growth after 2000, when the city went from 6,361 to 28,069 residents. Those lines are mostly plastic and relatively young. Older streets near the lake and downtown date from the 1960s and 1970s and are more likely to include cast iron or clay.

A sudden collapse may be covered in part by some homeowner or service line policies. Ask your carrier before work starts, and keep the camera footage.

Code and standards for sewer repairs

Sewer repairs fall under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted with state amendments under WAC 51-56, and under the rules of the sewer provider. In Lake Stevens that is the Lake Stevens Sewer District, which can tell you where the public main ends and your side sewer begins. Ask both the district and the City of Lake Stevens about permits before a dig.

Cast iron pipe repair and septic questions

Cast iron drain stacks inside the house can crack at hubs or rust through at the bottom of a run. A cracked section is cut out and replaced with plastic, using proper couplings. We also see homeowners ask about septic systems, and while most of Lake Stevens is on sewer, ask us if your property is not connected.

Questions we hear

Do you handle sewer emergencies at night or on weekends in Lake Stevens?

Yes. Our line answers 24/7. Stop using water, keep people out of the affected area and call so we can dispatch a crew.

How do I know if my sewer line needs repair rather than cleaning?

A camera inspection shows it. If a line keeps clogging after cleaning, or the camera shows cracks, roots or a sag, repair is the lasting fix.

Is pipe lining as good as replacement?

On pipe that is cracked but still round, lining is often a good fit. If the pipe has collapsed or sags badly, replacement is better.

Who is responsible for a broken sewer line, me or the district?

It depends on where the break sits. Ask the Lake Stevens Sewer District where its responsibility ends and yours begins.

Do you need to dig up my yard?

Not always. Lining and some spot repairs minimize digging, but collapsed or sagging pipe needs excavation.
