Plumbing Pipes for a Shower: A Lake Stevens Homeowner Guide

A shower runs on three sets of pipes: hot and cold supply, a drain and a vent. Knowing what each one does helps you plan a remodel, read a plumber's quote and spot problems early.

Call (360) 371-6092

By the Lake Stevens Pro Plumbing team / Published November 13, 2026

The pipes behind a shower wall

A shower needs hot and cold water supply lines, a mixing valve, a pipe up to the showerhead and a drain below the floor. The drain runs to a trap, which holds a water seal that blocks sewer gas, and the trap connects to a vent so the drain flows smoothly.

Supply pipes are small, typically one-half inch for a single shower. The drain is larger, usually two inches. The vent may run through the wall and up to the roof, or tie into another vent. If the vent is wrong, the shower gurgles and drains slowly even when the pipe is clear.

Drains are usually ABS or PVC plastic, and some older homes have cast iron. Our repiping page covers when supply lines should be replaced.

Questions to settle before walls are closed

  • Valve type and location: pressure-balance or thermostatic, at a height you can reach.
  • Access panel: a small panel behind the valve saves a lot of work if the valve needs service.
  • Drain size and slope: the pipe must slope toward the main drain, usually a quarter inch per foot.
  • Vent path: the vent must connect so the trap does not siphon.
  • Water pressure and temperature setting: a set temperature limit protects against scalds.

Rough-in versus finish plumbing

Rough-in happens before the walls are closed. The plumber sets the valve body, runs supply and drain pipes and tests for leaks. An inspection often comes at this stage. After tile and walls are done, finish plumbing adds the trim, showerhead and drain cover.

Moving a shower is more expensive than replacing one in the same place because the drain and vent must move. If you can keep the drain where it is, you save time and money. A plumber can tell you what a new location would involve.

Local note

Shower plumbing in Lake Stevens homes

Washington's state plumbing code, WAC 51-56, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with state amendments, and new shower plumbing is typically inspected before walls are closed. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department what your project needs.

Snohomish County PUD lists total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm for its Lake Stevens system, averaging 73. Water in that range can leave deposits on showerheads and valve cartridges over time, so choose a valve with easy-to-replace cartridges and keep the access panel.

Lake Stevens went from 6,361 residents in 2000 to 28,069 in 2010, and homes from that era often have PEX supply lines. A 1960s or 1970s house may have copper or galvanized lines inside the wall, and a shower remodel is a good moment to check them.

Mistakes that cause shower leaks later

Most shower plumbing failures start with a small mistake during installation. A valve set at the wrong depth makes the trim plate impossible to seat. A drain that is not sealed to the pan lets water creep under the floor. A joint that was never pressure tested can drip for months inside a wall before a stain shows on the ceiling below.

Ask for a pressure test of the supply lines before the wall is closed, and a flood test of the shower pan before tile goes down. Both are simple and both are cheap compared with opening a finished wall. Take photos of the pipes before the board goes up, so that years later you know exactly where the valve and lines sit.

Think about access, too. A shower valve with no access panel means any future repair requires cutting tile. A small panel on the back side of the wall, in a closet or hallway, can turn a major repair into a minor one. Plan it while the framing is still open.

Leak signs behind a shower wall

A damp ceiling below a second-floor shower, soft tile or a musty smell can mean a leak in the valve, the drain or the pan. Finding it early avoids mold and rot. Our leak detection service uses moisture meters and thermal imaging so walls are not opened until the source is clear.

Questions we hear

What size pipe feeds a shower?

Supply lines are usually one-half inch, and the drain is usually two inches. Your plumber confirms sizes for your layout.

Is PEX or copper better for a shower?

Both work. PEX is faster and more forgiving. Copper is rigid and has a long history.

Why does my shower drain slowly after cleaning?

A partial vent problem, a belly in the drain or a clog deeper in the line can slow flow even when the trap is clean.

Do I need a permit for shower plumbing in Lake Stevens?

New plumbing is often inspected. Ask the City of Lake Stevens building department about your project.

Can I move my shower to a new spot?

Yes, but the drain and vent must move too, which adds cost. A plumber can describe what a new location involves.
