How to Install a Toilet Flush Lever: A Lake Stevens Guide

A loose, sticky or broken flush lever is one of the easiest toilet repairs. It takes a few minutes and one tool, and it saves a call to a plumber if you get the chain right.

Call (360) 371-6092

By the Lake Stevens Pro Plumbing team / Published November 13, 2026

What a flush lever does

The flush lever is the handle on the tank. Inside the tank, it connects to an arm and a chain, and the chain lifts the flapper to release water into the bowl. If the lever is loose or the chain is too long or too short, the toilet may flush weakly, run or not flush at all.

Most levers are held by a mounting nut with reverse threads, so you turn it clockwise to loosen. A replacement lever costs little and comes in front-mount, side-mount and angle-mount styles that match the position on your tank.

Replacing the lever in five minutes

  1. Shut off waterClose the valve on the wall, flush and hold the lever until the tank empties.
  2. Remove the old leverUnhook the chain and unscrew the nut, remembering that it turns clockwise to loosen.
  3. Install the new leverSlide it through the hole, tighten the nut snugly without over-tightening and position the arm.
  4. Adjust the chainHook the chain with about half an inch of slack, then turn the water on and test.

Chain problems and fixes

SymptomCauseFix
Toilet runs after flushingChain too short, holding the flapper upAdd slack

Handle looseMounting nut not tightTighten it, turning counterclockwise from the inside

If a new lever does not fix the problem, the flapper or fill valve may be the culprit. Our toilet repair page covers those parts.

Check these before you buy a lever

  • Handle position: front, side or angle mount.
  • Lever arm length: a longer arm suits some tanks.
  • Material: plastic or metal in a finish that matches the room.
  • Tank hole: make sure the nut fits.
  • Chain length: some kits include a replacement chain.
Local note

Flush levers and Lake Stevens toilets

Federal rules limit toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush from 1994, so homes built since then use low-flow toilets that depend on a clean flush every time. A lever that does not lift the flapper fully can leave a bowl only partly flushed and cause repeat clogs.

Other tank parts worth a quick look

While the tank lid is off, check the flapper. A flapper that is warped, cracked or covered with slime will not seal, and the toilet will run or refill on its own at odd hours. Rub the flapper edge with your finger. If it feels rough or leaves residue, replace it. They are inexpensive and sold in a few standard sizes.

Check the fill valve and float. If the water level sits above the overflow tube, the fill valve is not shutting off. If it sits too low, the flush will be weak. Most fill valves have an adjustment screw or clip that sets the height.

Finally, put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait twenty minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. This small test finds the slow leaks that waste water for months before anyone notices.

When the problem is not the lever

If the toilet runs constantly, the flapper may be worn or the fill valve failing. If the bowl does not refill, a supply line or the shutoff valve may be clogged. If water leaks at the base, the wax ring has failed, which is a bigger job.

Questions we hear

How do I replace a toilet flush handle?

Shut off the water, empty the tank, unhook the chain, unscrew the mounting nut and install the new lever. Reattach the chain with slight slack.

Why does the nut turn the wrong way?

Flush lever nuts use reverse threads, so you turn them clockwise to loosen.

How much slack should the chain have?

About half an inch. Too little keeps the flapper open and the toilet runs. Too much prevents a full flush.

Can a bad lever cause a clog?

A weak flush can leave waste in the bowl, which may lead to more clogs.

When should I call a plumber?

If a new lever does not fix the issue, if the toilet leaks or if water runs after the tank is full.
