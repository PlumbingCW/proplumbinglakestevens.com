Toilet overflowing: what to do first

An overflowing toilet is urgent, and the first thirty seconds matter most. Our 24/7 line is staffed for it. Do these steps while you wait for a plumber.

  1. Lift the flapper. Take the tank lid off and lift the flapper, or close the float, to stop more water entering the bowl.
  2. Close the shutoff. Turn the valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  3. Do not flush again. A second flush sends more water over the rim.
  4. Call us. Tell dispatch whether other drains are slow. If they are, the clog is in the main line.

Toilet problems we repair

  • Constant running: a worn flapper, a bad fill valve or a chain that is too long.
  • Weak flush: blocked rim jets, a partial clog or a low water level in the tank.
  • Leak at the base: a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts, often with a soft floor.
  • Leak from the tank: cracked tank, bad gasket or tank bolts.
  • Wobbling toilet: loose bolts or a damaged flange.
  • Repeat clogs: a partial blockage deeper in the drain line.

Running toilets and leaks that waste water

A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day without making much noise. The flapper at the bottom of the tank wears out first, then the fill valve. Replacing both is usually quick and inexpensive.

A leak at the base is a different problem. The wax ring between the toilet and the flange has failed, letting water seep into the subfloor. We lift the toilet, inspect the flange and floor for damage, then reset it with a new ring and bolts.

Local note

Toilets in Lake Stevens homes

Homes built since the 1990s generally have toilets that use 1.6 gallons per flush or less, since federal law set that limit in 1994. Many Lake Stevens houses are newer than that, so a toilet that flushes weakly is more likely a clog or a flush-valve problem than a design limit.

Older homes near downtown and the lake can have older toilets with high water use and cast-iron drains behind them. In those houses, a toilet that clogs often points to scale in the drain pipe, so we check the line when we reset the toilet.

How a toilet installation goes

A new toilet is a small job done well or a big job done badly. The flange and floor are the difference.

  1. Shut off and drainWe close the supply, flush out the tank and disconnect the old toilet.
  2. Check the flangeWe inspect the flange and subfloor for rot or damage and repair as needed.
  3. Set the new unitA new wax ring, bolts and level setting keep it from rocking.
  4. TestWe connect the supply, flush several times and check for leaks.

Code and standards for toilets

Toilets are covered by the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted with state amendments in WAC 51-56, and by federal water-use limits that cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush. A replacement toilet is usually a like-for-like swap, but moving one to a new spot changes the drain and vent, and that is permit territory.

Questions we hear

Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Lake Stevens?

Yes. Our dispatch line answers 24/7. If the bowl is overflowing, close the shutoff valve behind it and call.

Why does my toilet keep running?

A worn flapper or a failing fill valve is the usual cause. Both are inexpensive to replace.

Can you fix a toilet that leaks at the base?

Yes. We replace the wax ring and bolts and check the flange and floor for damage.

How long does a toilet installation take?

A standard replacement usually takes about one to two hours.

Should I replace or repair a toilet?

If it is cracked or very old and wasteful, replace it. If a part has failed, repair is usually the better value.
