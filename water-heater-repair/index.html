No hot water or a leaking heater: do this first

A water heater problem is urgent when water is on the floor or the unit is making a loud hissing sound. Our line is open 24/7 for these calls. Until a plumber arrives, protect the house and keep everyone away from the tank if it is leaking near electrical parts.

  1. Shut off the cold supply. The valve sits on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Cut the power. Switch off the breaker for an electric heater. For gas, turn the control to pilot or off.
  3. Do not drain the tank onto carpet. If a hose is connected to the drain valve, run it to a floor drain or outside.
  4. Call the 24/7 line. Give us the brand, the fuel type and where the water is showing.

Diagnosing the problem before touching a part

Most no-hot-water calls come from one of a few failures. On electric heaters it is usually a burned heating element or a tripped high-limit switch. On gas heaters it is a pilot that will not stay lit, a failed thermocouple or a gas control valve that has quit.

We test before we replace. That means checking voltage at the elements, reading the thermopile or thermocouple output on gas units and looking at the anode rod and the bottom of the tank for sediment. A part that tests good stays in the heater.

Water heater symptoms and what they usually mean

  • Lukewarm water only: a failed lower element on an electric tank or a dip tube that has broken apart.
  • Popping or rumbling: sediment hardened at the bottom of the tank, with heat trapped under it.
  • Rusty or metallic hot water: a spent anode rod, and in some tanks, corrosion inside the steel.
  • Water at the base: a loose fitting we can tighten, or a tank that has rusted through and must be replaced.
  • Pilot keeps going out: a dirty pilot tube, a weak thermocouple or a draft pulling at the flame.

Gas, electric, hybrid and tankless units

Gas and electric tank heaters are the most common repairs. Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor and a fan, and a condensate drain that can clog and trigger a leak alarm. We handle those faults as well as the plain tank problems, and we will say so when a part is only available through the manufacturer.

Tankless units need descaling and burner cleaning more than they need parts. If yours shows an error code, call us with the code and model number. Our tankless water heater page covers flush and descale service in detail.

Local note

Water heaters and Lake Stevens tap water

Water in Lake Stevens comes through Snohomish County PUD, which buys most of it from the City of Everett's Spada Lake supply and adds water from its own wells. The PUD's water quality report lists total hardness between 9 and 89 ppm, with an average of 73. That spans soft to moderately hard, with the average at the low end of moderately hard, so scale builds up more slowly in tanks here than in very hard-water regions.

Slow scale is not zero scale. Draining a few quarts from the tank each year and checking the anode rod still extends tank life. Test your own tap and ask the PUD for its latest report if you want exact numbers for your street.

Code and standards behind water heater work

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), adopted in Washington with state amendments under WAC 51-56, covers water heater venting, relief valves and drain piping. The manufacturer's installation manual adds its own requirements, and a plumber follows both. A temperature and pressure relief valve that drips or has been tampered with is a safety issue, not a cosmetic one.

Hybrid heat pump water heaters and gas heater repairs

Hybrid and heat pump heaters are showing up in more Lake Stevens garages and utility closets. They need clear air space around the unit, a free-running condensate line and a clean air filter. A blocked filter is the most common cause of poor performance and is free to check.

On gas tanks, the venting matters as much as the burner. A draft hood or flue that has shifted can let combustion gases into the room, so we check venting whenever we service the burner. Anyone who smells gas should leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us.

Questions we hear

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Lake Stevens?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line any hour. If water is leaking, shut off the cold supply to the tank and the power or gas first, then call so we can arrive with the right parts.

How do I know if my water heater needs repair or replacement?

Age and the failed part decide it. A tank under 10 years old with a bad element or thermostat is usually worth repairing. A tank leaking from its body is not.

Why is my electric water heater only lukewarm?

A failed lower heating element is the most common cause. Tripped high-limit switches and broken dip tubes cause similar symptoms, so we test each before replacing parts.

Is my water too hard for a tank heater in Lake Stevens?

The PUD's own report lists hardness from 9 to 89 ppm, which spans soft to moderately hard. Scale still collects over time, so annual flushing helps, but severe hard-water damage is less likely here.

What should I do if I smell gas near the heater?

Leave the house, do not use switches or phones inside, and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Call us after the utility has cleared the house.
